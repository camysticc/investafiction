# Standards the design follows

Investafiction borrows ideas from two sets of published material and turns them into things the player has to do. It does not copy either document, and the rules inside the app are a teaching simplification. They must never be described as real procedure. Anyone wanting the real thing should read the official publications.

> Before release, check the short paraphrases below against the current published versions.

## Digital evidence principles

The digital evidence principles published by the NPCC for UK policing are summarised here in short form.

| Principle (short form) | How Investafiction applies it |
|---|---|
| 1. Do not change original data that might be relied on in court. | Originals are read-only. The player works on verified working copies. Each original is hashed at import. Trying to edit or rename an original is blocked and recorded as a serious procedural error. |
| 2. If someone must access original data, they must be competent and able to explain why and what the effect is. | Opening an original asks for a reason. The reason is stored in the custody log and scored. |
| 3. Keep an audit trail of everything done to the evidence. | The chain of custody and the action log record every step automatically, with a timestamp. The player can read both, and the report must be consistent with them. |
| 4. The person in charge is responsible for making sure the law and these principles are followed. | Before submitting, the player completes a legality, necessity and proportionality statement and signs off as case lead. |

## The National Intelligence Model

The model is built around an intelligence process: collect information, evaluate it, collate it, analyse it and pass on an assessment. It also describes analytical products such as network analysis and subject profiles. Investafiction follows the same flow: players grade their sources, pull evidence together in the timeline and link chart, and produce an assessment in the report.

The grading scales are simplified versions of the kind used in policing:

- **Source reliability:** A (always reliable) to E (untested).
- **Information confidence:** 1 (known to be true or confirmed) to 5 (suspected to be false).

These are not the official scales.

## Necessity and proportionality

Requests for data and authority, the OSINT rules and the traps are built on the general ideas of necessity, proportionality and data minimisation. They are simplified teaching rules. They are not a statement of the law and they are not legal advice.
